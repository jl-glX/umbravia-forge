# Legal readiness checklist

The legal pages in the application are working drafts, not legal advice. They intentionally avoid inventing business or personal information.

No private residential address has been published. The address and other
provider-identification details required for a future commercial release remain
explicitly pending professional advice and a suitable publication arrangement.

The account-lifecycle foundation also avoids defining binding retention periods. Its
technical boundaries and future review points are documented in
[Account lifecycle foundation](./ACCOUNT-LIFECYCLE.md).

An internally reviewed retention-policy version is operational metadata only.
It does not mean that its duration, purpose, legal basis or jurisdiction has
received professional legal approval, and it never enables destructive
execution by itself.

## Complete before public or commercial release

- Legal contact email.
- Business or professional address suitable for publication.
- Tax identification and registration details, when applicable.
- Final service description and territories.
- Prices, taxes, billing periods and renewal rules.
- Cancellation, withdrawal and refund conditions.
- Complete the maintained privacy-policy draft with the verified privacy
  contact, professional address, provider inventory, transfers and approved
  retention criteria.
- Cookie policy and consent mechanism if non-essential cookies are introduced.
- Processor agreements with hosting, email, analytics and payment providers.
- Stripe-specific responsibilities and customer-facing payment disclosures.
- Consumer complaint and dispute-resolution information.
- Professional legal review for the actual launch model and jurisdiction.
- Align the final privacy policy, terms of use and legal notice with the
  implemented account closure, recovery and retention behaviour.
- Validate separately the handling of account representation in cases of
  hospitalization, incapacity or death, including evidence, scopes, revocation
  and the rights of affected persons.
- Review deletion, anonymization, restriction and retention rules for account
  profiles, preferences, bookings, sessions, authentication factors,
  delegations, billing records and security events.
- Define who may approve legal holds, retention-policy versions and eventual
  deletion jobs, with auditable separation of duties.

## Maintenance

Update the displayed revision date only after reviewing the legal content. Material changes should be communicated to users and may require renewed acceptance.

## Official references reviewed for this technical draft

- [Article 10 of Spanish Law 34/2002 (LSSI)](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758), on information that an applicable service provider must make permanently, easily, directly and freely accessible.
- [Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj), especially transparency, storage limitation, data-subject rights and the limits of erasure.
- [Spanish Organic Law 3/2018 (LOPDGDD)](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2018-16673), including blocked data and requests concerning deceased persons.
- [AEPD guidance on information duties](https://www.aepd.es/preguntas-frecuentes/2-tus-obligaciones-como-responsable-del-tratamiento/6-el-deber-de-informacion/FAQ-0217-que-informacion-debe-facilitarse-cuando-los-datos-se-obtengan-directamente-del-afectado), including purposes, legal basis, retention criteria and rights.

These references support the checklist and transparency wording. They do not
replace the product-specific legal assessment still required before launch.
